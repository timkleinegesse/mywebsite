Chapter 1

Arrival: location, unpacking, assembly.

Most early damage happens in the first hour – while unpacking, carrying and assembling. These rules prevent it.

Location

The right place

Level floor (compensate for unevenness), enough room for functions, do not place directly against the wall (risk of mould), protect from direct sun and intense heat – otherwise the cover may fade and dry out. Ensure a balanced indoor climate.

Unpacking

No sharp objects

No knives or pointed objects on the packaging – the most common cause of cuts in the cover. Peel adhesive tape off the film by hand.

Transport

Lift, don't push

Always carry with two or more people, lifting only by the lower frame. Never push or pull – this strains feet and frame. Separate connected groups before moving them.

Element connectors

Hook in, press down

Place the elements side by side at the location, hook them in one after the other, lift and press down until the connectors engage. All connectors must be hooked in, otherwise height differences occur.

Grid connectors

Let them click into place

Fold the connectors out at right angles to the furniture and push the elements tightly together until the fittings click audibly into place and the gaps in the upholstery are closed. To release, lift the element upwards.

Feet & upholstery

Only at the final location

Fit the feet only once the suite is in its final position (follow the assembly instructions). Correct transport-related deformation of the upholstery by patting and smoothing.

Chapter 2

Care by material.

Basic rule for all covers: remove dust and dry dirt regularly. Never let liquids soak in – blot immediately with a clean, dry cloth, never rub.

Fabric covers

Vacuum with an upholstery nozzle at low suction in an even direction. A slightly damp duster against the pile direction can restore the elasticity of the fibres. For the collection also: professional cleaning, washable at 30°.

Care products

Use the commercially available care sets for fabric covers – they preserve appearance and lifespan. Stain removal itself belongs in professional hands: improper treatment is excluded from the warranty.

Wooden feet and wooden parts

Clean visible wooden parts with a slightly damp duster. Grain and colour variation are part of the natural material – differences even within one delivery are due to the wood.

Metal feet and runners

Clean with a slightly damp duster and polish off excess moisture. Treat brushed metal feet with the commercially available care products intended for them.

Protect the floor

Fit felt or plastic glides under the furniture feet, check screwed feet regularly for tightness and retighten if necessary.

Chapter 3

Everyday use: good to know.

Habits that keep upholstered furniture beautiful for a long time – and effects that are normal and not a defect.

Keeping it beautiful

  • Use all seats evenly – this spreads the wear.
  • Shake up loose seat and back cushions after use and pat them into shape; treat chambered cushions like a pillow.
  • After using a function, return the furniture to its starting position and plump up soft upholstery parts.
  • Do not sit on armrests, backrests or function parts, do not jump on the furniture.
  • With pets, put down a blanket as protection – claws can damage any cover surface.
  • Prevent static charge with higher humidity in the room.

Normal – not a defect

  • Initial furniture smell – disappears within the first few days, ventilate rooms well.
  • Seat shading and shimmering of the fabric depending on light and viewing angle.
  • Pilling from the fabric's own or foreign fibres – can be removed with a fabric shaver without damaging the fabric.
  • Folds and waves according to the upholstery character (taut / loose / extremely relaxed).
  • Different seat firmness on curves and function elements.
  • Seam offset after first use of a function – can be corrected by realigning.
Chapter 4

Packaging and old furniture.

The packaging protects the furniture until delivery – after that it becomes a recyclable material. Please dispose of it properly: correctly recycled packaging material can become a new product. And when a sofa has served its time after many years, it should be disposed of properly – not on the bulky waste pile in the yard.

Both are part of how we understand sustainability: asking with every decision how it affects the environment, society and the economy in the long term.
